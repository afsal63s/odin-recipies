Description

        Meatloaf stands as a quintessential comfort food, a hearty and
        satisfying dish enjoyed across generations. Its foundation lies in
        ground meat, most commonly a blend of beef, pork, and sometimes veal,
        chosen for their complementary flavors and textures. This meat is
        carefully combined with moistened breadcrumbs or other binding agents
        like soaked bread or oats, which help to keep the loaf tender and
        prevent it from becoming too dense. Eggs are essential as a binder,
        holding the mixture together during the baking process. Aromatic
        vegetables such as finely diced onions and minced garlic are often
        incorporated, adding depth and savory notes. The seasoning profile
        can vary widely, but typically includes salt, pepper, and a blend of
        dried herbs like parsley, thyme, and oregano, sometimes with a touch
        of paprika or Worcestershire sauce for added complexity. Shaped into
        a substantial loaf and placed in a baking pan, meatloaf is cooked in
        the oven until it's cooked through and nicely browned. A hallmark of
        many meatloaf recipes is a tangy-sweet glaze, often applied during the
        last part of baking. This glaze typically features ketchup or tomato
        sauce as a base, often enhanced with brown sugar, vinegar, or mustard,
        creating a flavorful and slightly sticky topping that complements the
        savory meat. Served warm, often sliced into thick portions, meatloaf
        is a versatile dish that pairs well with mashed potatoes, gravy,
        vegetables, or a simple side salad, making it a beloved staple in many
        home kitchens.
